We submit our original research article entitled **"Counterfactual validation of process simulation models: structural failures of single-metric credibility checks in an industrial penicillin fermentation benchmark"** for consideration in [Journal Name].

**The problem we address.** Mechanistic process simulation models are routinely accepted on the strength of a scalar accuracy figure — an end-of-batch RMSE, a trajectory-level normalised error, or an interval coverage — yet the capacity of those figures to distinguish a fitted model from a no-model baseline is seldom tested. Verification-and-validation theory requires that a validation exercise be able to falsify a model; identifiability theory requires that the observation operator resolve the states being fitted; reproducibility research has documented how leakage and degenerate metrics manufacture optimistic conclusions. These three literatures are rarely operationalised together, and the instruments used to accept a simulation model are almost never themselves audited.

**What we report.** We propose a portable **counterfactual validation protocol** that scores any validation criterion on three separately reported axes — control separation, output entropy and detection power — using zero-information controls, constant-detector tests, trigger-rate audits and observation-operator rank tests. We combine it with solver sensitivity analysis, identifiability diagnostics, leave-batch-out parameter transfer and discontinuity-aware sensitivity checks, and demonstrate the whole protocol on the publicly released IndPenSim benchmark (100 simulated fed-batch penicillin fermentations in a 100,000 L vessel), using a frozen 33-state mechanistic model and an 86-batch regression anchor with 2.3448% end-of-batch yield RMSE.

Four commonly used credibility checks are shown to be structurally non-discriminating: a residual-quantile coverage statistic returns 0.899549 for both a fitted model and a zero-information constant predictor; a legacy crash classifier returns `normal` for all 86 batches; a global innovation criterion flags 27 of 32 (84.38%) unperturbed batches as degraded; and a file-existence loading test is tautological. An admission filter rejects six of ten instruments, with reasons archived rather than deleted, and retains four for multi-criteria synthesis.

The accompanying numerical diagnostics bound what the headline accuracy can support. Re-integration of six representative batches with BDF, Radau, LSODA and fixed-step explicit RK45 gives a median end-of-batch deviation of 0.014% but batch-dependent deviations up to 32.567% near bistable basin boundaries, so numerical credibility is a structured, batch-dependent property rather than a single tolerance setting. Leave-batch-out transfer, in which held-out batches never supply their own calibration vector, raises RMSE to 97.47% (median prior) and 73.53% (drawn prior), with P5/P95 coverage of 0.22 — separating reconstruction consistency from conditional transferability. Propagation of nominal random effects over 30 fresh integrations gives a pooled yield-error RMSE of 35.95%, an order of magnitude above the reconstruction error. The four mycelial compartment states carry observation nullity 4 that is removed only by adding new measurement types, not by appending rows of an existing aggregate assay. The control-parameter response contains a 99.8–101.5 percentage-point discontinuity across which apparent finite-difference gradients diverge as 1/h, with 3 of 10 offline gradient-optimiser runs stalling at their starting values. Carbon balance closes within budget for 12 of 13 batches, while the approximately 12% simulated OUR/CER offset is retained as a declared model bias. The manuscript closes with a minimum admission filter and a reporting checklist for process simulation practice.

**Why this fits [Journal Name].** The contribution is a **validation methodology for process simulation models**, not an application report on a specific fermenter. We offer (i) an executable, machine-checkable definition of discriminative validity for validation criteria; (ii) a concrete, reproducible failure list of instruments in current use; (iii) a reformulation of structural non-identifiability and discontinuous parameter response as preconditions for simulation-based state estimation, sensitivity analysis and optimisation; (iv) a leave-batch-out experiment that answers the objection that per-batch calibration makes a low RMSE uninformative; and (v) a front-end admission filter that removes degenerate criteria before any multi-criteria decision synthesis. IndPenSim serves as an industrial-scale demonstration substrate. We therefore believe the work speaks directly to readers concerned with model credibility, verification and validation, numerical verification, identifiability and simulation-based optimisation. The negative results are deliberate methodological evidence, not a curated failure appendix: an instrument that cannot be wrong cannot validate anything.

**Scope and honesty.** The underlying study is unchanged. The model, the per-batch calibrated parameter table, the frozen baseline, the health assessment, the soft-sensor cross-validation, the candidate evaluations and every previously reported numerical result are identical; the additions are recomputations on the same frozen assets and supplementary numerical experiments requiring no new data. All evidence is in silico: we make no claim of prospective plant validation, wet-laboratory confirmation, GMP qualification, or bit-exact reproduction of the original MATLAB simulator (its stochastic seed was not recorded). Each validation batch uses its own calibrated parameter vector, so the 2.3448% figure quantifies reconstruction consistency, not prospective prediction; Section 3.5 reports leave-batch-out transfer explicitly so that this qualification is quantified rather than merely asserted. Software-engineering detail on the evidence-gating workflow, runtime loading, candidate evaluations and channel-level soft-sensor results is placed in the Supplementary Material so that the main text remains a methods paper.
